To remove text from a product video, first find out whether the words are still on an editable track. If they are, delete or replace the track in the source project and export again. If you have only a flattened video, choose between cropping, covering, rebuilding the shot, or trying local repair. The right choice depends on what the text covers and how much that part of the frame moves.

An expired discount in a corner is an easier job than a headline across a moving product. Identify any label, price disclosure, ingredient text, or legal copy that must remain before you make an edit. Use an authorized copy and keep the original available for direct comparison.

## Identify what kind of text you see

| **What you find** | **Best first action** | **Main risk** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A selectable title or lower third | Hide that track in a copy of the project | Removing a grouped element you still need |
| Text visible only in a subtitle-enabled player | Inspect the subtitle settings and file tracks | Mistaking a playback preference for a clean deliverable |
| Text burned into an expendable edge | Test a crop across the full shot | Losing packaging or changing framing |
| Static words against a stable background | Evaluate a fixed-area repair or intentional graphic cover | Visible patches, softened texture, or flicker |
| Text across moving packaging or essential copy | Find clean footage or recreate the shot | Inventing product detail the edit cannot recover |

Open the exact file you plan to edit outside the posting app. If the words appear only beside the video, they may be a post description or part of the platform interface. If a separate player can hide them with its subtitle controls, inspect the file before processing pixels. A subtitle setting changes what that player displays. It may leave the subtitle track in the deliverable.

When the words remain visible across players, check the source project or original camera file. Words physically printed on packaging are part of the recorded scene. If a legal line or label changed after filming, get an approved updated asset. Rebuilding a brand name or ingredient panel by inference risks giving the viewer false product information.

## Remove editable text in the source project

### Duplicate the editable project

Open the editing project that produced the product video and save a separate version for the new campaign. Keep the original project and its media linked. Move the playhead to a frame that shows the unwanted text, then inspect the video, title, graphics and caption tracks. A nested sequence or compound clip may hold the layer even when the main timeline shows only one item.

### Isolate and remove the overlay

Temporarily hide the suspected layer and check the preview. If the words disappear and the rest of the shot stays intact, delete the layer in the copy or replace its wording with approved text. Check the layer’s full duration. Promotional copy can repeat on an opening card, in a lower third, and again in an end card.

If hiding the track also removes a required logo or disclaimer, undo it and edit the individual object. For a product video with captions, leave required speech captions in place or keep a separate accessible version for the destination. Export a short sample first to catch a template that regenerates text or an alternate sequence that still includes it.

Check the first appearance of the text, the last frame it used to occupy and any transition around it. Verify the audio begins and ends where intended. Keep the editable project alongside the approved output so the next promotion can change without a repair pass.

## Crop or cover text on a flattened video

![CapCut’s desktop mask controls appear beside a preview and multiple tracks.](/uploads/remove-text-from-product-video/image3.png)

*CapCut’s desktop mask controls appear beside a preview and multiple tracks.*

[CapCut — Interface source](https://www.capcut.com/resource/remove-text-in-video)

Choose a crop when the text stays in an edge band and the subject has enough breathing room. Set the destination aspect ratio before choosing how much of the image to lose. A crop that works in landscape can cut off a product in a vertical social version. Scrub the full range, including the frame where the product gets closest to the edge. Reject the crop if it hides a feature the customer needs to see.

Covering an outdated badge with a deliberate graphic can make sense when a new price or approved caption belongs in that position. Add the new design as a track above the video, size it to cover the old pixels, and match its duration to the affected segment. Check entry and exit frames for a flash of the old words. A solid design element is often easier to judge than a soft blur over sharp packaging.

If neither route works, look for a clean alternate take, a short cutaway, or a product still you can use with permission. A shot can be replaced for the few seconds that contain the problem. Match the color and timing, and leave a little room before and after the replacement so the cuts feel intentional. This is particularly useful when the lettering passes over hands, reflections, or a label the viewer needs to read.

## Run a final product accuracy check

Review the export at the size a shopper will see and again at full resolution. A patch that disappears in a desktop preview can sit directly next to a product name on a small screen. Pay special attention to white or glossy packages, specular reflections and thin text. Assign someone who knows the product to compare the approved label with the video.

For a paid placement, also review the destination preview. It can crop the frame or position interface controls where your graphic now sits. Save versions with descriptive names so nobody accidentally uploads the source containing the expired offer.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I remove a product video text overlay for free

If the layer is still editable, your existing editor may let you remove it and re-export without an additional tool. Cropping may also work within your editor. A separate online repair service may require credits for video.

### Can I replace a price without changing the product shot

If the price is an editable object, update that object. If it is burned into the image, an approved cover graphic can work when it obscures the old price throughout its appearance and leaves the product visible.

### What if the text moves

A fixed selection stays in one place. If the lettering changes position or crosses important detail, use the source project, a different take, or a workflow designed for tracking and selective repair.

### Why does the result look good when paused but bad in motion

Individual frames can have plausible patches that differ slightly over time. Play the clip through the full affected interval and watch how reflections, texture and edges behave across the repair.

## Conclusion

Start with the editable project or the clean camera file. Use cropping or an approved overlay where the composition supports it. Reserve fixed-area repair for small stationary marks on authorized footage, and judge the saved video by what a customer can actually see.
