Want to know whether an image, video, or audio clip was made or edited with AI? The Google SynthID Detector checks for an invisible watermark that supported AI systems embed in generated media. The official Google SynthID Detector portal is now available globally in English, following Google’s October 2026 expansion. It can check supported media from Google and participating partners—but it is not a universal AI detector.

This guide explains where to find the official tool, how to check a file through the portal or Gemini, what the result actually means, and when an image watermark remover is a separate—not interchangeable—solution.

## Quick Answer: Where Can You Check a SynthID Watermark?

Open the official SynthID Detector online tool at [synthid.com](https://synthid.com/). Upload a supported image, video, or audio file and review the watermark-verification result. You can also upload a supported file to Gemini and ask, “Was this created or edited by Google AI?” Gemini’s SynthID checks currently focus on content from Google AI, while the separate portal has broader stated partner coverage.

A detected SynthID signal is evidence that participating AI technology generated or edited at least part of the media. No detected signal does not prove that the file is human-made, unedited, or authentic.

## What Is Google SynthID and How Does Its Detector Work?

SynthID is an AI content provenance technology developed by Google DeepMind. Instead of placing a visible badge on the corner of a picture, supported generation systems embed an imperceptible, machine-readable signal in the content itself. A Google SynthID watermark detector looks for that signal when you submit a file for verification.

![Illustrative scene: A camera next to a laptop displaying photo capture metadata. Not a screenshot of the named software or evidence of a completed product test.](/uploads/google-synthid-detector/image2.jpeg)

Google has described SynthID methods for images, video, audio, and text. That does not mean every public verification surface accepts all four formats: the consumer SynthID Detector portal described by Google supports images, video, and audio. Text watermark research and developer tools are a separate area, not a promise that you can upload a document to the portal and verify every paragraph.

### What kinds of AI-generated content can it identify?

The Google SynthID AI content detector is intended to identify supported watermarked media from Google AI systems and participating partner technologies. Google’s October 29, 2026 announcement names OpenAI, NVIDIA, Kakao, and Apple among partners; coverage still depends on which product and model applied SynthID to a particular file. It cannot automatically identify all images from every AI generator or tell whether a depicted event is true.

For example, a positive result on a heavily edited image can mean that an AI model was used during part of its creation or editing. It does not tell you who owns the image, whether the caption is accurate, or whether every element is AI-generated.

## How to Use the Google SynthID Detector Online

### Method 1: Check a File in the SynthID Detector Portal

1\. Keep the highest-quality original you can obtain. Save an untouched copy and note the source before verification; screenshots, re-exports, and repeated edits can change the evidence available.

2\. Go to synthid.com, review any displayed terms or access requirements, and select the supported image, video, or audio file you want to inspect. Only upload material you are allowed to share.

3\. Allow the tool to analyze the uploaded media. Depending on the media and current interface, it may indicate whether SynthID was detected or highlight likely watermarked segments or regions.

4\. Record the exact result and the date. Save a screenshot if the finding matters for a publication, review, or internal approval process.

5\. Cross-check with the original publisher, source history, any available Content Credentials, and other contextual evidence before drawing a conclusion.

### Method 2: Check Google AI Media Using Gemini

1\. Open gemini.google.com and sign in with a supported personal or eligible Workspace account.

2\. Choose Add files and upload one image, video, or audio file. Google’s Gemini help currently specifies a 100 MB maximum per file, videos shorter than 90 seconds, and audio shorter than one hour.

3\. Ask a direct question such as “Was this image created or edited by Google AI?” or “Does this video contain a SynthID watermark?”

4\. Read the verification explanation. If Gemini shows a positive signal, note whether the response refers to the whole file or a portion of the video. Check accompanying Content Credentials where available.

Google also documents approximate rolling 24-hour Gemini verification quotas: 10 image checks, 10 video checks (up to five minutes total), and 10 audio checks (up to three hours total). These are Gemini limits, not confirmed limits for the separate SynthID Detector portal; follow the current screen if quotas change.

### What if the portal will not open or cannot check your file?

First use the official link from Google DeepMind, then check whether the file type is supported and whether the current portal shows any account, size, or access restrictions. Try the best-quality source file rather than a compressed repost. If you only need to investigate Google AI content, Gemini is an alternative—but remember its narrower SynthID coverage. Do not treat a failed upload as a negative detection result.

## How to Interpret Google SynthID Detector Results

A detection answer is about a supported embedded signal, not a verdict on truth, copyright, or identity. Use the distinctions below when making an editorial or business decision.

![Illustrative scene: An illustrative provenance verification screen with a photo and file details. Not a screenshot of the named software or evidence of a completed product test.](/uploads/google-synthid-detector/image1.jpeg)

| Result | What you can reasonably conclude | What it does NOT prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SynthID detected | At least some supported AI generation or editing is associated with the content | Every pixel or frame was synthetic; the event depicted is false |
| No SynthID detected | The tool did not identify a supported signal in this checked file | The content is definitely human-created or no AI was involved |
| Inconclusive / could not verify | There is not enough reliable signal or the check could not be completed | That the file is authentic, fake, or safe to publish |

For video, Gemini may describe the segments where it detects a watermark; that does not necessarily establish that every frame in that segment contains a detectable signal. Keep the original file and the tool’s wording with any internal verification record.

## Google SynthID Detector Accuracy: What Are the Limits?

People searching for Google SynthID Detector accuracy often want a simple percentage. Google does not publish one universal accuracy rate that applies across every model, format, editing process, and upload surface. Its detection technology is designed to survive many ordinary transformations, but the outcome depends on the signal, supported source, and file quality.

![Illustrative scene: An image editor examining fine detail around the eye in a portrait. Not a screenshot of the named software or evidence of a completed product test.](/uploads/google-synthid-detector/image5.jpeg)

- Edits and compression: resizing, recoloring, cropping, or re-encoding may leave a watermark detectable, but extensive changes can make detection harder.
- Subtle AI edits: a very small adjustment may not include enough distinctive signal to produce a conclusive result.
- Coverage gaps: a different AI service may not use SynthID, or a partner may not watermark that specific output.
- Loss of original evidence: sharing-platform conversions or screenshots may remove metadata and complicate other provenance checks, even if some embedded signal survives.
- Meaning of a positive result: SynthID indicates a relationship to supported AI processing; it does not certify that the image subject or a news claim is true.

A useful verification workflow combines SynthID checking with reverse image search, source attribution, original-file metadata, and any trustworthy Content Credentials. Avoid claiming “100% AI detection” or “AI-free” based on a single scan.

## SynthID vs. Content Credentials vs. Visible Watermarks

These three terms are often mixed together, even though they answer different questions:

| Technology | What it is | Main use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SynthID | An embedded, generally imperceptible watermark in supported AI-generated or AI-edited media | Check whether a participating AI process left a detectable signal |
| Content Credentials (C2PA) | Signed provenance information that can describe origin and editing history | Review declared file history and the issuer of those claims |
| Visible watermark or logo | Text, branding, date stamps, or graphic overlays that people can see | Indicate attribution, labeling, or design elements within the image |

The Google SynthID detector is not a visible watermark eraser. Likewise, removing a visible label does not establish that an invisible SynthID signal has been removed. These are technically different operations.

## Google SynthID Detector FAQs

### Is the Google SynthID Detector the official Google DeepMind tool?

Yes. Google DeepMind links to synthid.com from its SynthID product page. The public portal was expanded globally in English in October 2026. For the Google SynthID Detector official website, start from those two Google-controlled destinations rather than an unofficial clone.

### Can the Google SynthID AI content detector identify every AI-generated image?

No. It looks for supported SynthID watermarks, which depend on the generator or editor that produced the file. A negative result is not a universal certificate that a picture was taken by a camera or made without AI.

### Does the online SynthID Detector work with AI-generated text?

Google has documented SynthID techniques for text, but its consumer detector portal is described for images, video, and audio. Do not assume the portal supports uploading written passages for reliable text-origin classification. Google’s developer documentation covers a separate SynthID Text approach.

### Can a SynthID watermark survive cropping, resizing, or compression?

Often, yes: Google says SynthID is designed to withstand common transformations such as resizing, recoloring, and compression. However, repeated or substantial edits can reduce detectability, so the best-quality original is preferable for verification.

### Can I use the Google SynthID Detector to prove a video is real?

No. A positive result points to supported AI processing, and a missing signal does not independently prove authenticity. Check the uploader, footage history, context, and corroborating sources before making factual claims.

### Can WatermarkGemini detect or remove a SynthID watermark?

WatermarkGemini is designed for visible image overlays, such as text, logos, and date stamps, in authorized files. It is not presented as a Google SynthID detector or an invisible SynthID remover. Use the official detector for provenance questions.
