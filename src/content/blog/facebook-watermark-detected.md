If Facebook displays a “watermark detected” message, first record the exact notice and where it appears. Then inspect the video you uploaded for visible third-party branding, an editor’s ending card or a mark already inside the footage. Re-exporting your own clean source is the most useful first correction when one of those is present.

Do not assume that every warning has the same cause. A watermark-related upload notice, an originality decision, a copyright match and an AI disclosure label are different issues. Removing a visible mark does not by itself establish ownership, reverse an account restriction or guarantee that a Reel becomes eligible for recommendations or monetization.

## Read the warning before changing the video

Take a screenshot of the complete notice, including any explanation, affected post and available review action. Record whether it appears while you upload, after publication, in a professional dashboard or in an account-status area. The stage matters because an upload suggestion and a final policy decision are not equivalent.

Look for the specific consequence described. Is Facebook asking you to review the clip, showing a content-level issue or limiting something at account level? Follow the actual notice rather than assuming a generic sequence of menu labels will appear for every Page and profile.

| What the interface describes | What to inspect | What removing a visible mark cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Watermark-related upload warning | The uploaded file and editor export | A guaranteed distribution outcome |
| Unoriginal or reused content | Source footage and meaningful creative contribution | Originality merely from a cleaner corner |
| Copyright or rights match | Rights information and the matched material | Permission to use protected work |
| AI information or disclosure | Applicable creation history and disclosure controls | That visible-logo removal changes provenance |
| Account or monetization restriction | The stated account decision and review options | That changing one video reverses the restriction |

These categories can coexist. An original video may contain a visible editing-app logo, and an unmarked video can still be reused content. Keep the problem you can actually observe separate from assumptions about Facebook’s internal detection system.

## Part 1 Diagnose the upload notice

### Step 1 Inspect the local file outside Facebook

Open the exact file you uploaded in a media player. Watch the beginning and ending and inspect the corners throughout the clip. A watermark can appear only during an intro or final card, so one clean opening frame is not enough.

Look for editing-app logos, third-party platform marks, usernames, template labels and old channel branding. Determine whether a visible name belongs to the player interface or to the video pixels. A mark that remains when the local file plays outside Facebook is part of that file.

Keep the uploaded filename and original project together. If you have several exports, compare their timestamps and duration. It is easy to upload an older branded file while inspecting a newer clean export with a nearly identical name.

### Step 2 Inspect the editing project and export settings

Open the project that produced the video. Check whether the mark is a removable title layer, a template element, a stock preview or an automatic ending card. When your plan or license provides an unbranded export, use that supported option rather than trying to repair an avoidable stamp afterward.

If the mark belongs to a stock asset you have not licensed, use the correct license or replace the asset. If it belongs to someone else’s source video, obtain an authorized clean version. A technically removable mark does not establish the rights needed for your intended use.

### Step 3 Separate evidence from guesses

If the local file has no visible mark, preserve the warning and the original rather than repeatedly cropping or degrading the clip. A notice alone does not reveal the detector’s threshold, the exact frame that triggered it or whether an unrelated issue is involved.

Do not rely on a claimed percentage, logo size or number of clean pixels as a guaranteed acceptance test. Avoid recipes promising that a small crop, metadata deletion or a specific re-encoding setting will always solve the warning. Those changes can damage a video without addressing the stated decision.

## Part 2 Re-export a clean original and upload once

When you control the original project, correct the visible issue at its source. This preserves more quality than repeatedly downloading, editing and recompressing an already compressed Reel. Keep the same underlying content revision so you can compare the result meaningfully.

### Step 1 Remove the unwanted export element at the source

Disable or remove the identified logo layer or optional ending card when you are entitled to do so. Replace unlicensed preview content with an approved source. Keep necessary credits or disclosures in the form required for the material rather than assuming the goal is to remove every identifying mark.

Check the entire timeline after the change. A watermark that returns during a transition or at the end can remain even when the main clip looks clean. Preserve captions and accessibility text that are useful to the audience; their presence alone does not make a video unoriginal.

### Step 2 Export and check the saved file

Use an export setting appropriate for the original footage and destination. Retain the intended vertical frame, full duration and soundtrack. Do not reduce the resolution or crop away meaningful information merely to make a corner less visible.

Play the saved file independently. Inspect its first frame, ending and intervals where the old mark appeared. Confirm that the file actually saved to the location you plan to upload from. Rename it clearly so it cannot be confused with the earlier branded version.

### Step 3 Check the upload and any resulting notice

Upload the corrected original through the account’s normal Reel workflow. Read the new result rather than assuming the absence of an immediate warning proves long-term eligibility. Preserve a record of what Facebook actually reports for that upload.

If a notice persists, use the available details, help or review action associated with the affected content. Avoid posting many slightly modified copies as a diagnostic method. One documented comparison between the old and corrected file is more useful than a series of untracked edits.

## Part 3 Address originality account and rights decisions

![Meta published interface example shows repetitive content across accounts. Removing a corner mark does not establish originality.](/uploads/blog-20261114/facebook-watermark-detected/figure-2.png)

*Meta published interface example shows repetitive content across accounts. Removing a corner mark does not establish originality. [Meta — interface source](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/04/cracking-down-spammy-content-facebook/)*

Meta’s current original-content guidance emphasizes material filmed or produced by the creator and meaningful creative contribution when other material is used. Cosmetic changes to reused material do not necessarily make it original. Removing a logo, adding a border or changing playback speed should not be presented as a substitute for creating or properly developing the content.

If the issue concerns originality, review what you contributed: original footage, analysis, explanation or substantial storytelling. Keep the project files and source recordings that show how the work was made. A clean local export can support your explanation but is not itself proof of the full production history.

If the issue concerns a rights match, read the match information and use the review or dispute process offered for that specific case. Gather the relevant permission or ownership evidence. Do not treat watermark cleanup as a way to resolve an ownership dispute, because the underlying matched footage or soundtrack can remain the same.

For account-level recommendation or monetization decisions, check the account’s available status and professional tools. Menu names and review options vary by account and rollout. Follow the action attached to the actual decision and preserve its wording instead of relying on a screenshot from an unrelated account.

### Keep AI labels separate from visible watermarks

![Meta policy illustration shows an AI information label as a separate transparency category; it is not the watermark warning.](/uploads/blog-20261114/facebook-watermark-detected/figure-3.png)

*Meta policy illustration shows an AI information label as a separate transparency category; it is not the watermark warning. [Meta — interface source](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/02/labeling-ai-generated-images-on-facebook-instagram-and-threads/)*

Meta uses AI-related information and disclosures as a separate transparency system. A visible corner logo and an AI label do not necessarily refer to the same thing. Read the applicable disclosure controls and describe the work accurately when a platform asks for that information.

Do not remove provenance information on the assumption that it is a guaranteed fix for a watermark warning. The practical first step remains to inspect the local video and the exact notice. If there is no visible mark, keep that distinction explicit in any report you submit through the available help or review route.

An owned video and its cover image are separate files. If the notice concerns the video, work from the original project and use Facebook’s available review process. Separately, you may have a still cover with an outdated campaign label or an event photograph with an old date stamp. Prefer the clean source or editable design. When an authorized still is flattened, WatermarkGemini’s image workflow may be useful for preparing that cover; it cannot diagnose a notice, repair the video or guarantee a platform decision.

- **Automatic visible-mark cleanup:** attempts to reconstruct the area occupied by an unwanted mark in a supported still.
- **Brush refinement:** when available for an eligible result, allows a further local cleanup attempt after reviewing its cost.

### Check the still before using it

1.  Open the [WatermarkGemini image workspace](https://www.watermarkgemini.com/) and select an authorized JPG, PNG or WebP cover image. Keep the original and inspect the preview for important edges and lettering.
2.  Sign in if required, check the available processing mode and the cost shown for the account, then start only if the option is appropriate. Image allowances and paid refinement may differ.
3.  Compare the returned still with the original. On a product cover, check the packaging and text; on an event cover, inspect faces and nearby texture. If a small area remains, check whether brush refinement is offered and what another attempt costs.

This optional cover preparation does not change the MP4, the notice, account status or any rights assessment. Keep a copy of the original image and the corrected video as separate assets.

## Check the new upload and keep your evidence

Keep the original recording, project, corrected export and notice screenshot together. Record which file was uploaded and what changed. This gives you a factual basis for a review request if one is offered and avoids confusing a local visual correction with a platform decision.

If the problem remains unexplained, describe the observed facts: the file is your own recording, the exact warning appeared at a specific stage, and a clean re-export did or did not change the notice. Do not claim to know which hidden detector fired. Continue through the available official help process with the relevant evidence.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does watermark detected mean my Reel is copyrighted by someone else

Not by itself. A watermark-related message and a rights match are different observations. Read the notice and any match information before deciding which issue applies.

### Will removing my logo restore monetization

There is no general guarantee. Monetization and recommendation decisions can depend on more than a visible mark. Correct the actual issue and use the review options provided for the specific decision.

### Why is my original video flagged when I cannot see a watermark

The notice does not reveal a universal cause. Check the exact uploaded file, its intro and ending, and whether you selected an older export. Preserve the original and warning if no mark is visible, then use available review or help options.

### Can I fix the warning by deleting metadata

Do not assume that. Visible watermarks, AI disclosure and provenance are separate issues, and no universal metadata-removal fix is established by the notice alone. Follow the platform’s applicable disclosure requirements.

## Conclusion

Start with the exact warning and the actual uploaded file. Correct visible export marks at the source, distinguish originality and rights issues, and preserve evidence for any available review. Use local cleanup only for an authorized editing need, with no promise that it changes Facebook’s eligibility decisions.
