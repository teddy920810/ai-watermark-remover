Choose a Gamma alternative by how the presentation will be edited and delivered. Canva fits teams that also produce social graphics and handouts. Pitch suits browser-first review. Beautiful.ai can help enforce layout rules, while Plus AI fits a team that wants AI drafting inside its existing slide editor. Google Slides and Microsoft PowerPoint are strong starting points when collaboration or an editable Office handoff is the main requirement.

Test the full route before committing to a subscription. An attractive preview says little about whether your customer can edit a table, whether a reviewer can comment without a paid seat, or whether your deck will run without internet access.

## Compare the tools by their strongest use

| **Alternative** | **Start here when** | **Check before committing** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canva | Decks share assets with campaigns and handouts | Premium assets and how a PPTX opens |
| Pitch | Colleagues review and present in a browser | Export access, branding and guest permissions |
| Beautiful.ai | Teams need consistent, rule-driven layouts | Trial terms and editable export availability |
| Plus AI | The team already works in Slides or PowerPoint | Add-in installation, plan and host access |
| Google Slides | Many people need to comment or edit | Font changes and round-trip PowerPoint behavior |
| Microsoft PowerPoint | The recipient needs an editable PPTX | Desktop requirements and Copilot entitlement |

Free access can refer to a browser editor, a time-limited trial, a limited generation allowance or the right to open someone else’s file. Compare the exact actions your team needs. A tool can be inexpensive for one presenter and costly for a workspace with several authors, reviewers and brand managers.

## 1 Canva for teams that make more than decks

![Canva’s editor with a presentation on the canvas and a template sidebar.](/uploads/gamma-alternatives/image1.png)

*Canva’s editor with a presentation on the canvas and a template sidebar.*

[Canva / Zapier — Interface source](https://zapier.com/blog/best-ai-presentation-maker/)

Canva makes sense when a slide is one part of a campaign. A team can use related colors, graphics and photos in a client deck, social post and printable handout without starting from a blank document every time. Its browser editor and template library are a good starting point for a small marketing team or a solo presenter who already works in Canva.

Start with a free template and a sample made entirely from assets your account can use. If the deck uses paid media, brand features or AI tools, check what your account includes before promising the workflow to the team. Export a short PowerPoint copy when a client needs Office. Open it in PowerPoint and try changing a headline. Compare spacing, fonts and imported elements with the Canva original. Use PDF when the recipient needs to review the appearance without editing individual objects.

## 2 Pitch for browser-based team review

![Pitch’s editable presentation canvas with slide thumbnails and a customer story example.](/uploads/gamma-alternatives/image2.png)

*Pitch’s editable presentation canvas with slide thumbnails and a customer story example.*

[Pitch / Zapier — Interface source](https://zapier.com/blog/best-ai-presentation-maker/)

Pitch is a practical Gamma alternative for a sales or product team that repeatedly reviews decks with colleagues. Put a real example through the collaboration workflow. Invite one reviewer with the access level you expect to use, ask them to leave feedback on a specific slide and confirm they can find the latest version without opening an older link.

Check Pitch’s current plan for workspace members, guests, export and branding before making it your delivery tool. A shared link can be an efficient working version, but a buyer may need a downloadable copy or a backup that works offline. If your team sends an editable deck to a partner, test that export and give the recipient clear instructions for which version to revise. Keep the source in the team workspace to avoid changes being split across multiple copies.

## 3 Beautiful.ai for consistent slide layouts

![Beautiful.ai’s presentation editor with a slide canvas and layout controls.](/uploads/gamma-alternatives/image3.png)

*Beautiful.ai’s presentation editor with a slide canvas and layout controls.*

[Beautiful.ai / Zapier — Interface source](https://zapier.com/blog/best-ai-presentation-maker/)

Beautiful.ai is useful when several people assemble presentations from a common set of layouts and the team prefers rules around spacing and alignment. It can save formatting work on recurring updates or standard proposals. Start with a slide that changes every week, such as a metrics table or a row of milestones. Add an unusually long label and check how the layout responds.

The same layout rules can limit a designer who needs exact placement. Test a dense appendix and a highly customized title slide before adopting it across a whole deck. Check the current plan and trial terms, along with the export types available for the account you would buy. Ask a colleague to reopen an exported sample and edit the table or chart. If your team mostly distributes view-only updates, a clean PDF may matter more than fully editable objects.

## 4 Plus AI for Google Slides or PowerPoint teams

![Plus AI’s remix controls for changing a slide layout.](/uploads/gamma-alternatives/image4.png)

*Plus AI’s remix controls for changing a slide layout.*

[Plus AI — Interface source](https://www.plusdocs.com/)

Plus AI is a fit when the team already has established PowerPoint or Google Slides workflows and wants help with the first draft, rewriting or layout changes. It operates as an add-in for supported host editors. Start with a representative brief or a file the organization allows you to use and build a few slides in the host that will hold the final deck.

The account must be able to install and use the add-in. A managed business or school account may require administrator approval. Confirm that your plan covers the host and action you need, and check what happens after a trial. The recipient can use the resulting presentation in its host workflow, but you still need to verify generated facts, chart data and how the deck looks on the delivery machine. Keep the editable slide file as the official version of record.

## 5 Google Slides for simple shared editing

![Google’s product illustration shows the Slides sharing and permissions interface.](/uploads/gamma-alternatives/image5.png)

*Google’s product illustration shows the Slides sharing and permissions interface.*

[Google — Interface source](https://workspace.google.com/products/slides/)

Google Slides is a useful starting point when the team already reviews work through browser links. Create a deck in the intended account, set the right permissions, and confirm that a reviewer can comment or edit as intended. For a group project or a cross-functional team, familiar collaboration can be worth more than one-click generation.

Check separately whether the account has the Gemini features you want. A team can use the editor for manual collaboration even when a particular AI feature is unavailable. If a customer requests PPTX, export and open that copy on the target machine. Check uncommon fonts, imported diagrams, speaker notes and slide transitions. If multiple recipients need different formats, keep a canonical Slides version and assign one owner to check every outgoing export.

## 6 Microsoft PowerPoint for editable Office delivery

![Microsoft’s PowerPoint page shows the application entry and a preview of its editor.](/uploads/gamma-alternatives/image6.png)

*Microsoft’s PowerPoint page shows the application entry and a preview of its editor.*

[Microsoft — Interface source](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/powerpoint)

PowerPoint is a good choice when the work ends as a PPTX that a client, teacher or presenter must edit. Start with the real slide master, fonts and chart conventions the recipient expects. Teams that present offline should rehearse from the saved file on the presentation computer. Embedded media and fonts can behave differently when the deck leaves the author’s device.

Check which PowerPoint features your web or desktop account includes. Copilot access can depend on a separate entitlement and account settings. If a customer expects a custom template or editable tables, verify those objects before generating a larger deck. Office-native delivery avoids one export boundary, but charts linked to external files and unavailable fonts can still cause problems. Share a PDF proof with the editable copy when matching the appearance matters.

## Test one real deck before switching

Use five slides from a recurring presentation. Include a title with your brand font, a table, a chart, a product photo and a slide with notes. Build or import them in the candidate tool, then ask one person who did not author them to review and edit the output. Test the workflow on the same device and account type that the team will use.

Compare the total effort required for the first draft, the corrections, and the final handoff. Include the cost of any paid export, AI credits, collaborator seats or required add-ins. If you need a file for an offline event, open that file with networking off before selecting the tool. Check the actual deliverable rather than relying on an export button being available.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a free Gamma alternative for team editing

Google Slides and Canva are useful starting points for evaluating basic editing access. Confirm which AI tools, assets and export options your account includes.

### Which tool is best for an editable PowerPoint file

PowerPoint itself is the natural baseline. For another editor, run a sample through export and verify that the recipient can edit the objects they care about.

### Can I move all Gamma features into another tool

Export and import can change the appearance or remove interactive behavior. Keep the original Gamma deck and use a test export to decide what needs rebuilding.

### Is an AI add-in better than a new presentation app

An add-in is a good fit when the team has a working host editor and wants help drafting. A new app makes more sense if the existing review, layout or publishing process is the main problem.

## Conclusion

Start with the recipient’s file and editing requirements. Test a representative deck, including review permissions and export, before you switch a team to a new workflow.
