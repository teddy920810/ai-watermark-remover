You can remove objects from photos on iPhone with Clean Up in Photos when your device supports Apple Intelligence. Open the picture, enter editing, choose Clean Up, and tap, brush, or circle the distraction. If your iPhone does not offer Clean Up, use a compatible photo editor or browser-based object remover instead.

Start by checking the exact iPhone model. An iPhone 15 Pro and a standard iPhone 15 do not have the same Apple Intelligence support, and installing a newer iOS version does not give an iPhone 13 the required hardware. The best route also depends on the picture: removing a tiny object against a wall is different from reconstructing a person standing across your main subject.

AT&T's device-specific iPhone 17e instructions include Clean Up in Photos. Apple's older requirements article explicitly lists the iPhone 15 Pro models and iPhone 16 models. For newer phones, use the current device documentation and the actual feature availability on your phone rather than treating that older list as an exhaustive catalog.

## Part 1 Check why Clean Up is available or missing

Clean Up is powered by Apple Intelligence. Hardware support, installed software, language, and region all matter. Apple's requirements identify iOS 18.1 as the original minimum on supported iPhones, with different rollout conditions in some regions. This is a compatibility threshold, not a promise that every later interface places the tool in exactly the same spot.

On an iPhone 15, check whether the model name includes Pro or Pro Max. A standard iPhone 15 or 15 Plus does not become a supported model by updating to iOS 18. On an iPhone 13, move directly to an alternative workflow rather than repeatedly searching Settings for a hidden Clean Up switch.

In iOS 27, Apple also documents Fast and High Quality Clean Up choices for different editing needs. If those choices appear, start with the mode suited to the target and compare the result rather than assuming the larger edit always needs the strongest option.

For an iPhone 17e, follow the device's Photos editing controls and allow any first-use preparation to finish. If the feature is missing, verify Apple Intelligence availability for the account, device, and region. If it appears but remains in preparation, check the connection and available storage before trying again.

Avoid drawing conclusions from a friend's screenshot alone. The app layout can differ across iOS releases, and regional availability can differ between otherwise similar phones. Search within the current Photos editing tools for Clean Up rather than installing an app that merely uses a similar name.

## Part 2 Remove an object with Clean Up in Photos

Use this route for a background passerby, a small bin beside a wall, or another distraction that the surrounding scene gives the tool a reasonable chance to reconstruct. Duplicate an important picture first if you want an immediately visible original and edited copy side by side.

### Steps in the Photos editing workflow

1.  Open Photos and select the original image. Let the full-resolution version load if the library is using optimized storage. A blurry preview makes selection and quality checks harder.
2.  Enter Edit and choose Clean Up. On versions with an additional tools panel, open that panel to find the feature. Wait for any required first-use download or preparation.
3.  Tap an automatically highlighted distraction, or brush or circle the object yourself. Cover its complete outline. Pinch with two fingers to zoom or pan when a small target is difficult to select.
4.  Inspect the new background before making another edit. If the fill bends a line or copies the wrong texture, undo that attempt and adjust the target rather than hiding the flaw with more strokes.
5.  Remove a remaining shadow or reflection only if it belongs to the distraction and should also disappear. Treat it as a separate visual decision; not every nearby dark shape belongs to the removed object.
6.  Tap Done when the edit is acceptable. Review the saved image in your library and preserve the original through a duplicate or Photos' available reversion controls.

Apple warns that brushing over a person's face may create a pixelated blur. If that happens, do not assume the tool has failed to download. You may have triggered face treatment rather than background reconstruction. Undo and select the appropriate whole distraction where permitted, or choose a different composition.

### What a good repair should preserve

Look at the place where the object touched the background. A person beside a railing can disappear while the railing becomes broken. A cable may vanish while leaving its shadow behind. These defects are easier to see at 100 percent, but the final decision should also include the size at which the image will actually be shared.

For a product photo, inspect the product's outline and printed information. For a travel image, follow rooflines, horizon lines, and paving. For a portrait, check that hair, hands, and clothing remain plausible. Generative reconstruction can produce a visually smooth region without reproducing what was really behind an obstruction.

## Part 4 Solve common problems without overediting

### The object is gone but the fill looks smeared

Undo and make a more deliberate selection that includes the full distraction without swallowing unrelated objects. Try one problem region at a time. If most of the background is obstructed, the tool has little reliable context; another photograph can be a better solution than a larger brush.

### You only need to remove something at the edge

Cropping may be simpler and more faithful than inpainting if the subject remains comfortably framed. Check the intended aspect ratio before saving. A crop that looks fine in your library may cut off the subject when a social platform applies a different frame.

### The edited image loses motion or looks different after saving

Confirm the output type. A downloaded PNG is a still image, even if its source was a Live Photo. Also compare the actual saved file rather than an on-screen preview; color handling, scaling, and compression in another app can change how it looks.

### You want to keep the person but remove the background

That is subject isolation rather than local object removal. Use a background tool and inspect hair, glass, and thin edges. A cutout cannot serve as a faithful reconstruction of the original environment.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I remove objects from iPhone photos for free

Clean Up does not require a separate retouching subscription on supported devices. Browser and third-party tools have their own trial, account, and download conditions. Verify the actual saving step before assuming a free upload means a free finished export.

### Does iOS 18 add Clean Up to every iPhone

No. The feature requires supported Apple Intelligence hardware and availability conditions. An iOS update alone does not add it to an iPhone 13 or standard iPhone 15.

### Can I erase an object without installing another app

Yes. Use Photos Clean Up on a supported phone, or a compatible browser editor. The browser option may still require an account and server processing.

### Will object removal restore the exact hidden background

No such result can be assumed. Reconstruction estimates the covered area. Keep an unedited original and avoid using an invented detail where factual accuracy matters.
