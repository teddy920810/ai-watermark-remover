Updated October 31, 2026 · Practical image verification guide

An unusually perfect portrait, an incredible moon shot, or a photo circulating with a dramatic claim can all raise the same question: how to tell if a photo is AI generated. The problem in 2026 is that realistic AI images may have flawless hands, readable text and convincing lighting. A visual “AI look” is no longer a reliable test.

The useful approach is to investigate the file and its context. Below, you will learn how to find a photo’s original source, check Content Credentials and Google’s SynthID signals, inspect suspicious details and interpret AI-detector results without mistaking clues for proof. The steps also work when you have only a screenshot, with important limitations.

## Quick Answer: How Can You Tell if a Photo Is AI Generated?

Start by tracing where the picture came from. Search for earlier copies with Google Lens, inspect the original file for Content Credentials or metadata, and use Gemini to check for supported AI-origin signals. Then examine the image for inconsistencies and compare the evidence. No single missing watermark, detector score or unusual hand proves that a photo is real or AI-generated.

![Illustrative scene: A laptop showing image matches during a reverse image search. Not a screenshot of the named software or evidence of a completed product test.](/uploads/how-to-tell-if-photo-is-ai-generated/image1.jpeg)

**1\.** Find the earliest credible source and any creator disclosure.

**2\.** Run a reverse image search to compare earlier versions and captions.

**3\.** Look for signed Content Credentials (C2PA), AI-edit labels and available file metadata.

**4\.** For Google AI images, try a SynthID check in Gemini; treat a negative result narrowly.

**5\.** Use visual details and third-party detectors only as secondary clues.

## 1\. Trace the Photo’s Original Source with Reverse Image Search

A picture can be a genuine camera photo and still be misleading if someone attaches the wrong location, date or event. Source tracing is the fastest way to separate questions about the image’s origin from claims made about it.

### How to reverse-search a photo with Google Lens

**1\.** On a computer, open Google Images or Google Lens, select the image-search option, and upload the best-quality copy you have. In Chrome, you can also right-click an online image and search with Google Lens.

**2\.** On a phone, open the Google app, tap the Lens icon, and select the photo or screenshot. Open “About this image” if the option is available in your results.

**3\.** Compare matching pages, captions, dates and image crops. Look for a creator’s original post, an earlier version, a trustworthy publication or an explicit AI-generation disclosure.

**4\.** If the picture supports a high-stakes claim, seek independent reporting or direct confirmation instead of relying on image matches alone.

Interpretation tip: a reverse-search result showing a different older caption can disprove the current story without proving the pixels were AI-generated. Likewise, a newly created AI image may have no web matches at all.

## 2\. Check Content Credentials, AI Labels and EXIF Metadata

![Illustrative scene: A camera next to a laptop displaying photo capture metadata. Not a screenshot of the named software or evidence of a completed product test.](/uploads/how-to-tell-if-photo-is-ai-generated/image4.jpeg)

### Verify Content Credentials (C2PA) in the original file

Content Credentials are tamper-evident records that some cameras, editing tools and AI systems attach to a file. They may identify the signer and indicate whether an image was captured, generated or edited using supported tools. They describe a recorded production history, not whether the depicted people or event are truthful.

**1\.** Obtain the original image file if possible, not a screenshot or a forwarded copy.

2\. Open the public Content Credentials verification service at [verify.contentauthenticity.org](https://verify.contentauthenticity.org/)

**3\.** Choose the file and read the available issuer, creation/edit actions and validation status. A verified AI-generation entry is evidence about that file’s origin; an empty or invalid result needs context.

If you use Google Photos on Android or iPhone, open the photo, select More > About, and look for “How this was made” where supported. Google says this section is not available for every photo and is not offered through Google Photos on the web. A separate “AI info” area may appear for certain metadata. Missing credentials never prove a picture is a real camera capture.

### Read EXIF data without treating it as proof

The original JPG or other supported image may contain a camera model, exposure, capture time or editing-software tags. On Windows, try Properties > Details; on a Mac, open the file in Preview and inspect available information; on a phone, use the Photos app’s information panel. The exact fields vary by file and device.

A plausible camera name is not a certificate of authenticity: EXIF fields can be edited and some AI images can contain camera-like metadata. Conversely, sharing, screenshots and file conversions often erase legitimate metadata. Use it to corroborate the source, not to make a verdict alone.

## 3\. Check for Google AI Signals with Gemini and SynthID

Google’s Gemini verification tools can examine supported media for SynthID, an invisible watermark associated with Google AI generation or editing. Gemini also supports Content Credentials checks on specified platforms, but the SynthID capability available in Gemini is not a universal detector for images from every AI provider.

![Illustrative scene: An illustrative provenance verification screen with a photo and file details. Not a screenshot of the named software or evidence of a completed product test.](/uploads/how-to-tell-if-photo-is-ai-generated/image5.jpeg)

### How to check a suspicious photo in Gemini

**1\.** Sign in at gemini.google.com using an eligible account.

**2\.** Use Add files > Upload files to attach one image. If checking a screenshot, crop tightly around the single image rather than submitting a collage.

**3\.** Ask: “Was this image created or edited by Google AI?” or “Can you verify any supported AI-origin signals in this image?”

**4\.** Read the explanation, including whether a supported watermark or Content Credentials was detected and any uncertainty reported.

A positive supported SynthID result means at least part of the content may have been created or edited with Google AI. No SynthID detected does not establish that an image is real or free of AI; other providers and unsupported files remain outside the test’s coverage. Do not upload intimate, confidential or sensitive images without permission.

## 4\. Look for Visual Signs of an AI-Generated Photo in 2026

If you are searching for “how to tell if photo is ai generated 2026 signs,” focus on consistency rather than a single cliché. Better generators can render convincing fingers, faces and signs, so the following are investigative prompts—not a checklist that automatically detects AI.

### Seven details worth zooming in on

- Text and logos: distorted letters, inconsistent spacing or a sign that becomes nonsensical when enlarged.

![Close-up of an illustrative AI-generated hand showing unusual finger geometry](/uploads/how-to-tell-if-photo-is-ai-generated/image6.png)

- Hands, ears and accessories: unusual finger contact, mismatched earrings, merged jewelry or glasses that do not connect logically.
- Reflections and shadows: a mirror that disagrees with the scene, or lighting that comes from incompatible directions.
- Background objects: repeating windows, impossible railings, duplicated people or objects that blend into one another.
- Skin and fine textures: waxy or uneven detail, inconsistent hair strands, or skin that changes abruptly near a boundary.
- Physical interactions: straps that pass through clothes, furniture with impossible joins, or objects that cannot be held as shown.
- Consistency across a set: a person’s facial geometry, clothing details or environment changing unexpectedly between supposed shots of the same moment.

![AI-generated hand illustration credited to Corvus CC BY-SA 4.0](/uploads/how-to-tell-if-photo-is-ai-generated/image7.png)

Existing illustrative AI-generated hand image: Corvus / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0. A visual anomaly is not proof of AI origin.

Important: real photographs can look strange because of motion blur, lens distortion, digital zoom, portrait mode, compression or conventional retouching. Treat several consistent anomalies as a reason to verify the source—not permission to accuse the creator of fabrication.

## 5\. Use AI Image Detectors as a Second Opinion, Not a Verdict

Some online AI image detectors classify patterns in pixels and output a label or a probability-like score. Their performance depends on the generators they were trained against, the image type and changes such as cropping or recompression. A “95% AI” label is not a verified 95% chance that the particular photo is fake.

### How to interpret conflicting detector results

**1\.** Save the original and note which file version you tested.

**2\.** Check whether the service identifies specific supported provenance signals or merely estimates an AI likelihood from visual patterns.

**3\.** If tools disagree, record the disagreement and prioritize original-source evidence, signed provenance and trustworthy corroboration.

**4\.** Avoid uploading sensitive photos to an unfamiliar detector; review its privacy and retention terms first.

A useful outcome is often “origin unconfirmed.” That is more accurate than forcing a yes/no answer from a classifier that has not been validated for your specific image.

## 6\. Check Special Photo Types: Portraits, Moon Shots and Pregnancy Tests

![Illustrative scene: A person comparing a print portrait with a digital version on a monitor. Not a screenshot of the named software or evidence of a completed product test.](/uploads/how-to-tell-if-photo-is-ai-generated/image8.jpeg)

### How to Tell if a Portrait Photo Is AI Generated

Portraits are particularly hard to evaluate because realistic AI faces can look natural even at high resolution. Compare multiple photos from the same account or shoot, look for credible original posting history, and inspect clothing edges, earrings, glasses, hairline, lighting and background geometry. If the image is presented as a real person, seek an independent profile or other direct evidence rather than making judgments based on skin texture or facial features alone.

For an alleged portrait of a public figure, a known authentic photo can help test mismatched details, but angle, age, makeup and editing can create differences. A verified source or credible correction is stronger evidence than a visual similarity test.

### How to Tell if a Moon Photo Is AI Generated

A highly detailed moon photo is not automatically AI-generated. Modern phones may combine exposures, apply computational sharpening or enhance recognized scenes; those processes differ from generating an image from a prompt. Ask for the original file, device information and capture context. Compare the visible phase, orientation and surrounding sky with independently documented observations only when that comparison is relevant and reliable.

If a seller or poster claims an unedited camera capture, examine what they mean by “unedited.” Computational photography and AI-based enhancement are not the same as a fully synthetic image. Metadata may support the account but cannot prove it.

### How to Tell if a Pregnancy Test Photo Is AI Generated

You cannot verify a pregnancy result—or confirm who took a test—from the appearance of a photo. Packaging, printed text, fingers, surface reflections and visible overlays can be edited, staged or generated. Reverse-searching may reveal a recycled image, but a new image with no matches is not necessarily authentic.

If the result has personal or medical consequences, rely on direct communication, an appropriately performed test and professional medical advice when needed. Do not use a pixel classifier to validate a health claim or the identity of a person in the photo.

## 7\. Decide What the Evidence Actually Supports

| **Evidence available** | **Careful conclusion** | **What to do next** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verified AI-generation credential or supported watermark | Evidence supports AI generation/editing for the indicated system or portion | Describe the signal and its scope, and retain the original file |
| Credible camera source and consistent provenance | Evidence supports a camera-origin account | Check whether later AI editing or false context is still possible |
| Conflicting detector results, no credentials, or only visual clues | Origin remains unconfirmed | Request the original, search for independent evidence, or avoid a firm claim |

Distinguish three different questions: Was this image generated? Was a genuine photo edited? Is the accompanying story true? The evidence needed for each can differ. Even a genuine camera capture can misrepresent reality, and even valid Content Credentials do not independently establish factual accuracy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you tell if a photo is AI-generated just by looking at it?

Not reliably. Odd hands, text and reflections can be useful clues, but modern AI images may look fully natural and real photos may contain optical or editing artifacts. Check the source, signed provenance and supported verification signals as well.

### What is the best way to tell if a photo is AI-generated in 2026?

Use a combination: trace the original creator, reverse-search the image, inspect Content Credentials and EXIF information, and check for supported SynthID signals in Gemini when relevant. No single step covers every model or file.

### Does no AI watermark mean the photo is real?

No. A file may have been generated by a system that does not use the watermark a checker recognizes. Credentials may also be missing or stripped. “Not detected” means only that a supported signal was not found.

### Can Google Lens tell whether an image was made with AI?

Google Lens can help you find image matches, earlier contexts and About this image information. That may reveal an original source or available provenance indicators, but a reverse-search result alone does not definitively classify an image as AI-generated.

### How can I check a screenshot when I do not have the original photo?

Crop it tightly to the image, run a reverse image search and ask the poster for the original. Gemini may check supported signals in an uploaded screenshot, but screenshots can discard metadata and reduce the evidence available for verification.

### What is the difference between an AI-generated photo and an AI-edited photo?

An AI-generated image may be synthesized largely from a prompt. An AI-edited photo can start as a camera capture and have certain regions generated, removed or altered. Credentials and source history may help identify which occurred, but they may not capture every edit.

### Are AI image detector scores accurate enough to use as proof?

No. A score depends on the tool and image type and may be affected by compression or editing. Without transparent validation for the relevant images, a numerical score should be treated as a lead to investigate, not proof.

### Can WatermarkGemini detect AI images or remove invisible SynthID watermarks?

No. WatermarkGemini is for authorized cleanup of visible image overlays. It does not determine whether a photo is AI-generated and should not be presented as removing invisible AI provenance signals.

## Conclusion

The best answer to “how to tell if a photo is AI generated” comes from several independent pieces of evidence. Start with the source, check for signed creation and editing history, use Google-specific verification where appropriate, and treat visual errors or classifier scores as supporting clues. When the origin cannot be established, say so. If an authorized image separately needs visible text or watermark cleanup, keep that editing task distinct from authenticity verification.
